# Report — chapter 16

## Retell
It is the first day of the rice cut, eight days after the yard scene. The first terrace step is gold and dry, and the whole hill is cutting it. Children crack clappers at sparrows, and women set up threshing boards. Private Renshu stands on the wall shouting that every grain goes to the granary on the captain's order. Kesa carries her father's ledger, and her father works the weighing beam. The terrace folk now treat her as the garrison's girl.

- Nobody will speak to her. The terrace boy's mother keeps beating her sheaf. The old pipe man puts his cold pipe in his mouth. A second-step man spits by her foot. The hen woman cuts off her apology with "Twelve eggs" and turns her back.
- Her cousin Yan says privately that "Garrison's girl!" in the gate was meant: "I meant it. Every word." She also says "Don't come up the ninth." Kesa wants to promise "I'll bring you the night" but can't, because a private's spear-butt is knocking along the wall ten paces away.
- At noon, during the weighing, the captain hands Kesa a sealed order and has her read it aloud to the elders. Its terms:
  - Each step is cut in its own week, weighed on its own wall, and its sacks are carried to the granary the same day under escort.
  - Any step that lends the ninth step "a hand, a sack or a boat shares the ninth step's fire."
  - If any step fails its share, the garrison sends to the fort, three days upriver.
  - The ninth's dry week comes out at about day 100, and the captain agrees.
  - From the night of the second step's cut (about seven days away), the terrace path is shut from last lamp to first light.
  - A forty-first lamp is hung at the sluice at the foot of the hill, to be lit by "the lamp girl, Kesa."
- Kesa reads it in a flat voice. She deliberately slows down on the path-shutting line so every elder hears the seven days. At the end the whole hill turns and looks at her "the way you look at a post when you have seen what it is for."

The world, as I read it: Earth Kingdom rice terraces under Fire Nation occupation. The garrison rules by lamps and curfew, and the hill is quietly planning to cut and ship its rice in one night.

## How this world works, as far as I can tell
- Each terrace is cut in its own week, bottom to top, because ripe rice "drops" its grain. — (stated: "Rice left standing past its week let go of its grain into the mud")
- The hill has always cut with shared labour: "every step lent the step below it its hands." — (stated)
- The garrison's share is weighed on the wall on cut day and carried to the granary under escort. — (stated, in the order)
- Collective punishment is the garrison's threat. If any step fails, a hawk goes to the fort and soldiers arrive in three days, and all nine steps suffer. — (stated). The elders would be hanged from granary beams. — (stated, as a memory of the captain's earlier talk)
- The terrace path will be closed at night once the second step is cut. — (stated). I take it that nobody can travel up or down in the dark after that. — (guess)
- A lamp at the sluice will light the only way down off the hill, so anyone moving sacks will be seen. — (stated, as Kesa's reading). My own reading is the same, that it's a watch-light. — (guess)
- Kesa's fire is small and exact at night. — (stated in earlier chapters; here only echoed by the old general's line). Her noon fire is dangerous. — (stated earlier). Neither is used in this chapter.
- The cutting date for the ninth is public now: about the hundredth day. The captain's "noon" is therefore presumably around then. — (guess). The exact night before it is still not known to anyone on the hill.
- I could not tell whether the captain's noon-burn of the ninth is still happening. The order says only that the ninth "stands to its own dry week." The burning comes from my memory of earlier chapters, not from this page.

## Who's who
- Kesa (also Hua): main character, lamp girl, half Fire Nation. She reads the captain's order to the hill in a flat voice.
- Father (Stores): works the weighing beam and chalks the count. Kesa calls "One, two…" and his lips shape the numbers a breath after her. He said "Captain's" once about her assignment.
- Mother (Bai): not present. Her bowl and her board are only mentioned.
- Captain Shuren: homeland officer who smooths her glove finger by finger. She has Kesa read the order publicly, calls her "Hua," and says "Thank you, Hua."
- Private Renshu: loud and boastful. He guards the first step and says "I'm a wall myself."
- Yan: Kesa's cousin, standing in for her father (the ninth's elder). She says "I meant it. Every word." and "Don't come up the ninth."
- Gao: Kesa's uncle, the ninth's elder. He is not present: "a gap at the end of a wall."
- Hen woman: carries a hen and is the grandmother of the jailed boy. She answers Kesa's apology with "Twelve eggs" and turns her back. She also asks aloud "Ninth's when?"
- Old man with the pipe: second-step elder. He says "They did it upriver."
- Terrace boy's mother: her son was taken in irons. She beats sheaves hard and won't answer Kesa.
- First step's elder: bow-legged. He cuts the first sheaf and says "It drops."
- Second-step man: spits by Kesa's foot.
- General (old man at the port): mentioned only in a memory line.

## What the main character wants, and what happens if they fail
Kesa wants to learn the night before the captain's noon and get it to the hill, so the hill can cut and ship the ninth's rice before it is burned. If she fails, the ninth burns, and the order's wording implies the other steps burn with it and the elders hang. She also wants the hill to stop hating her, but that seems secondary.

I can't tell how she means to do it. After this chapter it is harder: she can't speak to anyone, the path closes at night after seven days, and the lamp at the sluice will be hers.

## Terms I had to guess
- bund — my guess: the raised earth edge or walkway beside a terrace field — first seen: "Along the bund the women were setting out the threshing boards"
- coping — my guess: the flat top of a wall — first seen: "He slapped the coping"
- sluice — my guess: the stone gate or cut where the hill's water channels exit at the bottom — first seen: "where the channels came off the hill into their stone cut at the foot of the hill"
- "the count" — my guess: an earlier weighing or measuring of the green crop, a month ago — first seen: "the old man who had cut the sample row at the count"
- the fort — my guess: a larger Fire Nation garrison three days upriver — first seen: "the garrison sends to the fort"
- "They did it upriver" — my guess: the same hanging or burning has already been done at another valley or town — first seen: "'They did it upriver,' he said."
- the "ninth's place" — my guess: the empty seat of the missing ninth elder — first seen: "Yan stood at the end of them, in the ninth's place"
- "shares the ninth step's fire" — my guess: gets the same burning, and probably punishment — first seen: "Any step that lends the ninth step a hand, a sack or a boat shares the ninth step's fire."
- "the night. Of the second step's cut" — my guess: the night when the second step's rice is cut, roughly seven days from now. I'm fairly sure of it but not certain, because "from the night" reads oddly — first seen: "From the night. Of the second step's cut. The terrace path is shut."

## Where I was confused
- "Any step that lends the ninth step a hand, a sack or a boat" — Kesa reacts as if the captain has guessed the harvest plan, because of "boat," then says it may just be thoroughness. I couldn't tell if the captain knows. Whether she does is the biggest open question and I couldn't resolve it.
- "From the night. Of the second step's cut. The terrace path is shut. From the last lamp. To first light." — I got the gist, but the chopped sentences made me re-read. I also wasn't sure whether it is shut every night from then until the end of harvest, or only that one night. I took it as every night (guess).
- "A hundred, less thirty-four: sixty-six days" and "ninety-sixth day… hundred and first" — I followed it on the second pass, but I'm not sure why 16 markets became 80 days and the day count started at 16 and not 0. I trust the arithmetic without having checked it.
- "The pine post… beside it lay a coil of chain. Lamp chain" — I didn't know what this was for until the order was read. I guessed it was a lamp mount, and I was right, but it sat unexplained for most of the chapter.
- "Nobody on this hill had a boat, nor ever had" — I understood that Kesa had arranged boats through Fen. I only half-followed why the captain writing "boat" would be a clue.
- "They did it upriver" — I don't know what "it" refers to or when this happened.
- Why Yan says "Don't come up the ninth" — protection (guess), a rebuff, or a warning she'll be watched. I can't tell.

## Where I skimmed
- "Eight days since the yard. She had lit her round every dusk of them, docks first…" — a long recap paragraph. It reminded me of things I already knew (market day, the bowl with a plate over it, "Good night, Hua").
- "A hundred, less thirty-four: sixty-six days from this wall to the ninth, two months and a little more." — the date arithmetic ran longer than I cared to follow, though I wanted the result.
- "Kesa had carried it for eight days. Since the slate, since the man on the well rope had said nine…" — again a re-run of earlier beats before the hen woman scene.
- The paragraph explaining the cutting schedule ("Rice left standing past its week let go of its grain…") — useful, but it slowed down a scene that was moving.

## Best moment
"They were looking at her now the way you look at a post when you have seen what it is for." — it pays off the "post somebody had put up in the night" image used earlier in the chapter, and it makes the new lamp feel like a trap. It lands the hill's hostility and the captain's cleverness in one line. Close second is the earlier beat where Kesa reads the shut-path line "at a walking pace… setting each word down on the wall in front of them like a sack" so the elders can't say they didn't hear it. It shows her quietly working against the order while reading it.

## What I expect next, and what I want to know
- Kesa will use the sluice lamp as a signal. It is the one fire she controls and the whole hill will see. I expect "small fire that lasts the night" to pay off there (guess).
- The second step is cut in about seven days, so the path shuts. I expect a tight week where Kesa finds some way to tell the hill something, possibly through her mother's fish board or Yan.
- Dao will turn up again and help, or look away once more.
- Open questions: does the captain know about the strip, Fen, or the boats? Why are the hill's people certain Kesa is really the garrison's girl? Is there any sign Yan has forgiven her? Where is the prince going with all that pitch?
- I would also like to know what the captain actually plans for the "noon." The order now sounds more like a trap closing than a burning.

## Would I click next?
4 — the stakes are clear and rising, with the hill turned against Kesa and the plan boxed in. The date arithmetic and recaps cost me a little patience.
