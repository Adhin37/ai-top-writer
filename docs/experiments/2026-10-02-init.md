## Seed, and why this one

> In the city of Varrow every promise that matters is sworn on a bell. Loans, marriages, guild
> contracts: you swear on your bell, the Chime keeps it in the bell-house. Break your word and your
> bell cracks, and you lose your voice for good. The voiceless end up in the Lowquarter doing work
> nobody else will. Ness repairs bells for the Chime. Her brother's bell cracked last month for a
> debt he swears he never swore, and now he can't tell her what happened. She has a secret: she can
> hear when a bell is lying. For Royal Road readers who like a clever main character, clear rules
> and a slow climb.

Chosen because it is unlike everything the room has seen. The slice novel is low-magic political
intrigue about surveyors. The knowledge bases' invented worlds are a sealed habitat where air is
billed and a harbour town that rows out its drowned. Its central rule is a mechanism with a visible
cost in the street (a voiceless underclass), which is what a webnovel-clear chapter 1 needs. It is
a Royal Road shape (a clever protagonist, a mystery, a climb), and it names readers' tastes
rather than an author to imitate, so round 5 has to do its job.

## Answer sheet

Written before init started; not changed after. Each answer in the log says its source: `sheet`,
or `rec — sheet silent`.

- **Round 1, seed and platform.** Royal Road. Rating teen: violence on the page is fine, nothing
  sexual. Hard limit: no sexual violence. The story in one line: a bell-mender proves her brother's
  bell was cracked by a debt he never swore, and finds out who is ringing false oaths onto the
  voiceless.
- **Round 2, genre, tone, protagonist.** Fantasy. Grounded, with dry humour; people help each
  other, at a price. One viewpoint, close third, past tense. Ness is eighteen, an apprentice
  bell-mender, sharp and stubborn, quicker with metal than with people. She wants her brother's
  voice back and his name cleared; what she needs is to stop doing everything alone. Her gift is
  hearing a bell lie. **Its cost must come from how hearing works**, not nosebleeds or fainting. She
  is not reborn and knows nothing of the future. Modules: mystery clues on. Everything else, the
  recommendation.
- **Round 3, world.** The rule as in the seed. The Chime casts, keeps and rings the bells, and
  gets rich on oaths: every loan in Varrow is sworn through it. The voiceless have their own hand
  language and their own quarter; they are not pitied, they are used. One city. Keep it small.
- **Round 4, cast.** The antagonist is a person: the Chime's master of the bell-house, who believes
  the bells are the only honest thing in Varrow and would crack a hundred innocent bells to keep it
  so. Their face on the page by chapter 2 at the latest. Allies: her brother (voiceless now), a
  voiceless fixer from the Lowquarter, a Chime apprentice who starts to doubt. No romance for now.
- **Round 5, style.** Plain and quick, clear enough to follow cold, with dry humour. Not literary.
  If no sample has the humour, ask for a mix: the clearest sample, with the humour of the wryest.
- **Round 6, premise.** Say-back by the showrunner: read *In one breath* once, then write it from
  memory in its own words.
- **Round 7, title and plan.** Take the recommended title unless it is grandiose or a noun stack.
  The ending: she breaks the Chime's hold on the bells and her brother speaks again, but it costs
  her the gift. Everything else, the recommendation.
- **Anything not covered:** the recommendation, logged as `rec — sheet silent`.
