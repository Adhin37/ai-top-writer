# The world's own clock

The world does things whether or not the protagonist is there, and `plan/timeline.md` keeps them:
the opposition's plan, the standing clocks (a season, an audit, a debt falling due), and pressures
that do not care who anyone is. Each actor that reacts has a profile: how fast it answers
(**latency**) and how accurately it reads what the protagonist did (**fidelity**). The gap between
an action and the world's answer is where dread lives. A world needs at least two kinds of actor in
play, and one of them should not react at all.

## Examples

### Profiles that differ

| actor | profile | latency | fidelity | what it wants |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the Stack council | institution: opens a file, answers with policy | 10+ chapters | high, from the record, not the person | its numbers to balance |
| Kell | strategist: watches before moving | 3–6 chapters | high | his own record clean |
| Security's night chief | chaotic: fast and personal | 1–2 chapters | low: blames the wrong fitter | someone to blame tonight |
| the Deck Nine pump | indifferent: failing on its own schedule | — | — | nothing |

### Fidelity makes plot

> Ch 6: Tovi forges a chit to get her brother extra air. Ch 8: Security, reading the stores log,
> blames Ossie, whose signature is on the next line.

The night chief misread what she did. Now she must decide whether to correct the record and lose
herself, or let Ossie pay. A world that always reads the protagonist correctly gives her nothing to
decide.

### A clock the reader can see

> Ch 1: Tovi's gauge reads eleven days of air. Ch 3: eleven. Ch 5: eleven.

A clock nobody looks at again is a date. Give the threat one visible step a chapter, on the page:
the gauge at nine in chapter 3, and in chapter 5 the reading she was afraid of, in a room she did not
think to check.

### Latency is preparation time

The council's file was opened in chapter 4 and answers in chapter 15. Between them the reader
knows it is coming and Tovi does not, and every ordinary scene has that file under it.

### The governor

If two reactions are due in the same chapter, delay the slower one. If the protagonist is losing on
every front at once for three chapters, a reaction misfires or arrives late. The world is
indifferent to the protagonist; the plan is not indifferent to the reader.

## When to break it

An actor may react instantly once, when the speed itself is the reveal ("they knew before she got
back to her bunk"). That tells the reader the actor was watching all along, and it costs the
surprise only once.
