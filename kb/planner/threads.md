# Threads, foreshadowing and payoffs

Every thread is a promise on the board (`state/threads.md`) and a row in the ledger's *Promises*,
with a chapter it is opened, the chapters that advance it, and the one that pays it. Plant a payoff
three times, each less subtle than the last: ambient, doing another job; noticed by someone, who
moves on; loaded, at a tense moment, unexplained. A payoff should surprise and feel inevitable, cost
someone something, change the situation, and open a smaller, sharper question as it closes a large
one.

## Examples

### Three plants for one reveal

The reveal (ch 12): Kell has been sending fitters to the deep crews on purpose.

```
ch 3   ambient   the recruiter greets Kell by his first name in the corridor; nobody remarks on it
ch 7   noticed   Ossie: "Funny, the recruiter knew Kell." Tovi: "Everybody knows Kell." They move on
ch 10  loaded    on the recruitment ledger, three names in Kell's handwriting, at the moment Tovi is
                 hiding from Security
ch 12  paid      Kell's own log of the grades he struck, matched to the ledger
```

Once, and the reveal feels arbitrary; four times, and it is telegraphed. The second plant matters
most: the reader remembers having been offered it.

### A payoff that changes nothing

> Ch 12: Tovi learns the recruiter is Kell's brother. Ch 13: she carries on as before.

That is trivia. Plan what it re-prices: a relationship (Ossie trusted the recruiter), a decision now
forced (she must tell the deep crew she leads), or an earlier cost paid for nothing.

### A promise moved, not dropped

```
| R4 | who struck the fitters before Tovi | 3 | moved to ch 14 — ch 12 needs the room for the ledger; ch 14 pays it with the widow from ch 3 | 
```

A promise past its chapter is moved with a reason, never left to age silently. A promise the reader
will not get back (a dead end) is paid by someone saying so on the page.

### Close large, open small

The arc's big question closes; a smaller, sharper one opens in the same scene, so the reader has
something to carry over the arc break.

## When to break it

An unexplained detail planted for no reason yet (an object, a scar, a name) is a gift to later
chapters. Plant one now and then, log it as `planned` with a far chapter, and let a later beat sheet
find the use.
