# A fair-play mystery

Fair play means the reader had everything they needed. The pleasure is being fooled fairly and
seeing, afterwards, exactly where the answer was. Keep a clue ledger beside the thread board, with
what the **reader** holds and what the **protagonist** holds as separate columns, because the gap
between them is the mystery's tension. Hide a clue by giving it another job, never by hiding it:
a clue nobody could notice is a clue the reader skimmed.

## Examples

### The ledger

| id | clue | planted | means | disguised as | reader has | protagonist has |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Kell signs for clamps an hour before leaks | ch 2 | he knows which mains will fail | a paperwork beat in a busy scene | ch 2 | ch 4 |
| K2 | the recruiter calls Kell by his first name | ch 3 | they are kin | a greeting in a corridor | ch 3 | ch 12 |
| K3 | three ledger names in Kell's hand | ch 10 | he picks who goes down | a hiding scene, the names glimpsed | ch 10 | ch 10 |

From chapter 3 to chapter 12, the reader holds K2 and Tovi does not. That is dramatic irony, and it
is planned.

### One clue, two readings

> The recruiter greets Kell by his first name.

Fully explained by the innocent reading (they have worked on the same deck for twenty years) and
fully explained by the true one. The best clue is one the reader has already accepted as true.

### Too many fingerprints in one scene

> Ch 2: Kell stands in front of the one locked store, lends Tovi's family air on credit nobody
> asked for, holds a promotion over a fitter, and says he is sorry about the brother before anyone
> told him.

Each is a fair clue, and four of them in one scene solve the mystery by chapter 2. Plant one sign a
scene, and give the culprit a scene in which he is right, at a price he pays himself, so the reader
has to weigh him rather than file him.

### A red herring that plays fair

A false trail is fair only if it is genuinely suggestive, it is cleared on the page by something
the reader can see, and clearing it teaches them something true.

### The solution

Every step of the answer uses a clue in the ledger, planted before the reveal, and the decisive one
early, in the first third, before the reader is suspicious.

## When to break it

A story that is not a puzzle (whose question is "what will she do", not "who did it") needs only
the general reveal discipline in [threads.md](threads.md), not a ledger.
