# Arcs and chapter rows

A chapter row in `plan/chapters.md` is a set of decisions made **before** drafting: the event, the
temperature, the kind of ending, the cost. An arc rises, turns at a midpoint, cools, and rises again
to a climax that has somewhere to rise from, and the cool stretch is not optional. A big moment is
built up over several chapters before it is spent. Vary temperature and endings so the reader cannot
predict the last line, and keep about ten rows planned ahead, never the whole book.

## Examples

### A row that decides

```
| 5 | The Second One | Tovi Brand | tense | reveal | sign onto a deep crew before her credit runs low | the recruiter wants her brother's chit, not hers | she was not the first fitter Kell struck this way | Tovi signs the deep-crew contract in her brother's name | she now owes ninety days below | Ossie walks her to Deck Seven, unasked |
```

Event: one clause a reader could retell. Temperature and ending: set now, so the chapter is written
to them. Cost and keeps: both concrete.

### Temperatures across a stretch

```
ch   temp        hook
4    tense       decision
5    tense       reveal
6    loud        cliff
7    quiet       question    ← the cool chapter after the blow; it still has a want and a turn
8    warm        arrival
9    procedural  threat
```

Never the same temperature three running; four or more different ones across an arc. Ending kinds:
reveal, arrival, decision, question, threat, reversal, cliff (rare, about once in ten), quiet
(once an arc, after a heavy chapter). No kind more than twice in any five.

### Built up, then spent

**Spent at once:**

> Ch 4: Tovi learns the deep crews do not come back. Ch 5: she signs.

**Built up:**

> Ch 2: a poster with the return count painted over. Ch 3: a fitter's widow on Deck Seven. Ch 4:
> the recruiter's ledger, open on his desk, a column of names with no return date. Ch 5: she signs.

The second makes chapter 5's signature cost something, because the reader has been gathering the
price for three chapters.

### The skim test

For every row, name the one thing a reader would lose by skipping the chapter. If you cannot,
merge it with its neighbour.

### The rate the opening promises

Chapters 1–3 promise a rate as well as a kind: how much happens in a chapter, and how long a
problem stays open. A reader who stays has subscribed at that rate, and what loses them later is
rarely "too slow". It is "it changed".

**An opening faster than the book:**

> Ch 1–3: each chapter raises a problem and settles it. Arc 2: one question runs for twelve
> chapters.

**An opening at the book's rate:**

> Ch 1: Tovi's grade is struck and her thirty days start. Ch 2–3: the days run, and every fix she
> tries makes them shorter. The reader learns this is a book where one problem runs an arc.

Plan the opening at the rate the arcs can keep, not the rate that converts best.

### Replanning

When a chapter's page has moved the story off its row (a character the reader loves, a promise
the reader is holding), rewrite the next rows to use it rather than steering back. Keep the event
of a row unless the reader ledger needs it changed, and say so in a `changed` line.

## When to break it

A climax can run several chapters at one temperature (loud, loud, loud) because the reader has been
promised it. Plan the cool chapter right after it.
