# The beat sheet

One page, written in story language: what happens, in order; what the reader learns and at which
moment; what they should feel by the end of each scene. The writer stages it; the story editor
judges the draft against it. Constraints belong in the few header lines. Everything below them is
the story.

## Example

The world is invented.

```markdown
# Ch 1 — "Thirty Days"
event    Supervisor Kell strikes Tovi's grade in front of her crew, for a leak she reported
temp     tense      hook  decision
learns   P1 — the leaking main: Tovi tells the new hire where all air comes from, while she clamps it
         P2 — shift change: her meter ticks over, billed, the moment the grade is struck
         P3 — the clerk reads the thirty-day rule off the form; her brother asks what day 31 means
faces    Kell — her supervisor, twenty years on the Stack; wipes his hands on a clean rag before he
         touches anyone's paperwork
promises made: R1 (why Kell did it)
pressure L1 set — she logs the fault in her own name, knowing whose shift it was · C1 set — the
         clerk reads her thirty days off the form
cost     her grade, and her brother's allowance with it
keeps    the new hire, Ossie, stays after shift to help her carry her kit — nobody asked him to
voices   Tovi → bible/cast/tovi-brand.md · Kell → bible/cast/_voices.md · Ossie → bible/cast/_extras.md

## Scene 1 — the Deck Four air main; Tovi, Ossie, two fitters
goal: clamp the leak before the deck alarm trips / obstacle: the clamp is the wrong gauge /
turn: she fixes it with a borrowed part and logs the fault in her own name
- the hiss, the frost on the pipe, Ossie useless and eager
- Tovi explains, working, that every breath on the Stack comes up this pipe from Deck Nine (P1)
- she logs the fault — the right thing, and the reader should feel it as the right thing
- reader learns: P1
- feel: competence; this is someone who keeps people breathing

## Scene 2 — the shift office; Tovi, Kell, the crew watching  <- the event, longest scene
goal: collect the crew's shift chits / obstacle: Kell has her fault log and a different story /
turn: he strikes her grade for "causing" the leak, and makes her watch him file it
- Kell's rag, his calm; the crew not meeting her eyes
- the strike; her meter ticks over at shift change — billed, now, for the first time (P2)
- the clerk reads her the thirty-day rule off the form (P3)
- reader learns: P2, P3; meets Kell (A1)
- feel: the floor dropping; injustice with a clock on it

## Scene 3 — the family bunk; Tovi, her brother
- he asks what day thirty-one means; she answers it straight, because he is nine and deserves it
- she takes the deep-crew recruitment slip out of her pocket and does not throw it away

## Ending
The slip on the table between them. The question: will she take the job nobody comes back from?
```

## Checks

- **The event is one clause a reader could retell** — a concrete verb and a target — and it sits in
  the longest scene.
- **Every `learns` item names its moment and who carries it**: someone saying it, someone doing it,
  or the protagonist weighing it before a decision. At least one lands in the first scene. *"P3 —
  in free indirect: grades are set once a year, no appeal"* names no one, and a rule nobody carries
  is the paragraph a reader skims. *"P3 — the clerk reads the thirty-day rule off the form"* names
  the clerk.
- **Every scene turns**: something is different at its end. A scene that only delivers information
  is a bridge — make it a paragraph of summary, or give it a turn.
- **`feel` is an experience**, not a theme: "the floor dropping", not "injustice is explored".
- **`pressure` names the lead's move as a deed on the page**, and it is bigger or costs more than
  her last step in the ledger's L1 trail. `held` is a cool chapter's, never two running. The
  clock comes nearer or holds; a line that pushes it later spends the reader's patience, so it
  needs a reason the reader is given. When the dispatch says `Pressure:`, that row is what this
  chapter answers.
- **`keeps` is small and real** — something a reader would not want taken away. A chapter that
  only takes is not tense; it is an instalment plan.
