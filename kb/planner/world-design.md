# Designing the world

A world earns its pages through one **central rule** and everything that rule does to ordinary life:
who works, what things cost, what the law allows, what people believe, who may move. Write the
smallest bible that answers those questions, with every price as days of an ordinary wage and every
distance as days of travel, because those are the units a reader feels. A power or a technology has
a cost and a limit, and its second-order effects show up in the street before they show up in a
battle.

## Examples

### The rule reaching ordinary life

**A rule that stays in the sky:**

> The Stack is a sealed habitat. Air is produced on Deck Nine.

**The same rule, followed down:**

> - labour: your grade decides how much of your air is paid; the deep crews are the only work for the
>   ungraded, and they are paid in air, not credits
> - money: credits buy food and bunk space; air is billed separately, every shift, and debt is
>   counted in days of air
> - law: lose your grade and you get thirty days of air on credit; on day thirty-one you go down-well
> - belief: nobody on the Stack says "breathe easy"; it is an insult
> - mobility: moving decks needs a grade stamp; the ungraded live where their grade left them
> - news: the shift bell carries notices; anything else travels by word, slowly, deck by deck

Six lines, and a writer can stage a hundred scenes from them.

### Prices in days of work

```
anchor     a graded fitter earns 10 credits a shift; a shift is a day
a meal     2 credits (a fifth of a day)
a bunk     30 credits a week
a clamp    40 credits: four days, which is why fitters borrow
an appeal  300 credits: a month, which is why nobody appeals
```

Five prices, held forever. A scene where someone pays an appeal fee now means something.

### Distances in days, with what slows them

| from → to | days | slower when | in between |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deck Four → Deck Nine | half a day by ladder | the lifts are rationed at shift change | the pump hall, the grade checkpoints |

### A power with a cost and a limit

> The deep crews' suits recycle air for ninety days. Limit: they cannot be refilled outside Deck
> Nine. Cost: after a tour, a body that has breathed recycled air for ninety days cannot breathe the
> Stack's air comfortably for a month, so a returning crew is visible on every deck it walks.

The limit makes plans; the cost makes scenes.

### One speculation, followed

If the world rests on one invented technology or power, name its second-order effect on ordinary
life and use that instead of the spectacle: if air is billed, then breath-holding is a habit of the
poor, and a child who holds his breath on the ladder is a character note.

### An oath the plot can stand on

An oath binds a character the reader believes will not break it, so a plot can be built on it
holding. Design the words, who witnesses, and what the breaker loses.

> A fitter signs a gauge reading under the words "my reading is my breath". A false reading costs
> the grade, and no fitter on the deck takes that person's word again. Kell signed one, nine years
> ago. Ossie's father witnessed it.

Now a character can be trapped by their own integrity, which the reader cannot wish away the way
they wish away a locked door.

## When to break it

A setting the reader already knows (a real city, a borrowed canon) needs less bible, and its central
rule is whatever the story changes about it. Write only that, and its consequences.
