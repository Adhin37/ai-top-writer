# Stakes

Every chapter costs something, and every arc costs more. Small costs most chapters, a medium one
every five or so, a large one each arc. Escalate **what is at risk**, not how strong the opponent
is: from the protagonist's own safety, up through what they built, who they are responsible for,
who they love, what they believe about themselves, to what others need that only they can protect.
Reader interest follows the **gap** between protagonist and opposition, not its size. And no
consequence may land before the reader has seen what it costs someone.

## Examples

### The ladder, not the treadmill

**Escalating the opponent:**

> Arc 1: a supervisor. Arc 2: the deck chief. Arc 3: the Stack council. Arc 4: whoever runs the
> council.

**Escalating the stake:**

> Arc 1: Tovi's grade (her own standing). Arc 2: her brother's air (someone she is responsible for).
> Arc 3: the deep crew she now leads (people who need what only she can give). The opponent in arc 3
> can be a single clerk.

A rung-5 threat from a clerk beats a rung-1 threat from a god.

### The gap, chapter by chapter

```
ch 4   Tovi vs Kell's shift office          outmatched (+1): she wins only by paying the brother's allowance
ch 5   Tovi vs the recruiter                even (0): a trade, both sides give something
ch 6   Tovi vs Security                     hopeless (+2): she loses, and it opens the arc
```

Mostly outmatched, sometimes even, rarely hopeless, almost never easy. Three chapters at the same gap
read as a flat stretch whatever happens in them.

### The stakes ceiling

> Ch 2: Tovi mentions the Deck Nine pump in the canteen. Ch 3: Security has her name. Ch 4: she is
> arrested for sabotage.

The reader has not yet seen what Security does to anyone, so the arrest reads as plot rather than
dread. Schedule the bystander first: in chapter 3, a fitter the reader has met is taken by Security
for less. Now chapter 4's arrest has a price.

### The step she pays for

> Ch 6: Ossie has kept three pressure logs for a year, sealed and dated, and shows Tovi all three:
> the readings were faked at the main.

The investigation's biggest step, handed over complete by the second person asked; the protagonist
pays nothing and the reader is only told. Give her half: Ossie has kept one log; the other two are
on Deck Nine, in a fitter's locker, and getting them costs Tovi a shift she cannot spare. An ally
who helps is welcome. An ally who finishes the protagonist's work is a shortcut.

### What the chapter gives back

Every row has a `keeps` beside its `cost`: something small and real the reader would not want taken,
best when it is unearned. A plan where every chapter only takes is relentless, not tense.

## When to break it

An arc may hold the stake still and widen the gap instead (the same brother, a harder enemy) when the
reader needs to see the protagonist's competence tested. Do that one arc at a time, never both at once.
