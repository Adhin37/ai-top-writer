# Bias in the design

**This is the one area whose rules are absolute.** They override genre convention, a user's tropes
and any reference material. Prejudice can be depicted, examined and suffered; it cannot be the
book's own view of the world. In the design that means: no people, species or nation carries one
moral trait; every named woman wants something that is not about the protagonist and pursues it on
the page; and who is competent, who is ignorant and who is rewarded does not line up with a group.
The line-level half (descriptions, epithets, who gets a thought) is the line editor's.

## The rules

- **A faction has interests, internal disagreement and dissenters.** Write its case in two
  sentences, as it would put it.
- **No invented people is evil by nature**, and no physical marker signals moral quality.
- **The agency test:** for every named woman, and every named man, what do they want that is not
  about the protagonist?
- **Read the competence grid as a block.** If the people who cannot read, fight, count or plan line
  up with a gender, class or nation, that pattern is the book's argument. Re-deal which narrowness
  goes to whom; where the world itself bars someone, show who barred them and let it cost the
  barrier something.
- **Intelligence is never carried by a demographic marker.** The slower characters a cast needs are
  not the accent, the servant or the foreigner by default.
- **Sexual violence is not a plot device.** If a story genuinely requires it, it is off the page,
  and its consequences belong to the person it happened to.

## Examples

**A faction as one trait:**

> The Down-wellers: brutish, resentful, envious of the Stack's air.

**A faction with a case:**

> The Down-wellers: the families of the deep crews, who have been promised a return for forty years.
> Their council wants the return count published; their younger members want the Deck Nine valves.
> In their own words: "The Stack breathes because we don't."

**A competence grid that argues something by accident:**

```
can read the Stack code:   Kell, the council, the recruiter       (all graded, all upper-deck)
cannot:                    Tovi's mother, Ossie, the canteen staff (all lower-deck)
```

Re-deal: Ossie reads the code better than Kell, and has never been asked.

**What a society expects, and what each person does about it.** The expectation is one rule for a
whole group; what each character does about it is not. Give each character who carries scenes a
stance toward it: conforms and benefits, conforms and resents, exploits it, defies it openly,
defies it quietly, never had to notice, or enforces it. Any character of any gender takes any
stance.

> On the Stack a son sits the grade exam and a daughter is graded on her father's word. Kell, who
> has three daughters, enforces it harder than the council does. Tovi's mother sat the exam under
> her brother's name and never said so. Ossie has never had to notice.

## When it does not apply

A character's prejudice, voiced and shown costing someone, is depiction. Keep it. Check only that
the plan does not reward it, and that the faction it targets has its own case somewhere on the page.
