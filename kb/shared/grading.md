# Grading a retell

Grade each owed fact against what the reader **wrote**, not against what they might have meant.
The question is whether this reader could repeat the fact to a friend.

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| **stated** | the retell gives the fact in substance — the *mechanism*, not just its name — as something the page said |
| **partly** | the retell names the thing, or gets part of the mechanism, or marks the right answer as a guess |
| **missing** | the fact is absent from the retell and from the reader's list of world rules |
| **wrong** | the retell contradicts it, or confidently gives a wrong guess |

Quote the words you graded. When two sections of the report disagree (the retell misses a fact the
rules list states), grade the better one and say so.

## Examples

Owed fact **P1**: *Once a year the sea at Merrow gives back everyone it has drowned; each must be
rowed out past the harbour bar by dawn, or they stay.*

| the reader wrote | grade | why |
|---|---|---|
| "Every year the sea sends the drowned back and somebody has to row them out again before morning, or they stay in town." (stated) | **stated** | the mechanism, in the reader's own words |
| "There's a yearly thing called the Long Ebb where the dead come back somehow." | **partly** | the name and the return, without the rowing-out or the deadline |
| "Some kind of tide festival where she ferries people (guess)." | **wrong** | a confident wrong mechanism — the dead are not in it |
| (nothing about the sea or the dead) | **missing** | |
| "The dead come back and have to be rowed out by dawn (guess)." | **partly** | right, but the reader had to guess it — the page did not say it plainly enough |

The last row is the one to watch in chapter 1: a correct guess is still a fact the reader had to
work out alone, and most readers would not have.
