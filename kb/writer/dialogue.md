# Dialogue

People talk in fragments, interrupt, answer the question they wish they had been asked, and stop
before the end of the thought. Each speaker's voice comes from their row in `bible/cast/_voices.md`
— how fast they think, how well they say it, how they read people — so two people in one scene
should not sound alike even with the tags removed. Not every exchange is a duel: allies agree,
joke, get distracted, and are wrong together.

## Examples

**Prepared statements — every line a finished paragraph:**

> "I understand your concern, but the regulations are clear, and I cannot make an exception for
> you, however much I might personally wish to."
> "Then I suppose I have no choice but to find another way to secure the air my brother needs."

**Spoken — interrupted, uneven, with the real subject underneath:**

> "Regulations are—"
> "I know what they are."
> The clerk tapped the form. "Then you know I can't."
> "He's nine."
> "Grade's a grade." He slid the form back across the desk, and did not quite let go of it.

The second version says less and means more: the clerk's hand on the form is the scene.

**Two people who are not opponents:**

> "You're doing the thing," Doran said.
> "What thing."
> "The thing where you count the boats twice." He passed her the flask. "There's six. There were six
> an hour ago."
> "Seven, last year."
> He didn't have an answer for that, so he didn't try.

## When to break it

A character who speaks in finished paragraphs can be *characterised* by it — the lecturer, the
official, the one who rehearsed. Then it belongs to them alone, and somebody else in the scene
should notice it.
