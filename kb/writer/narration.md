# Narration

**Distance** is how far the prose sits from the viewpoint character's mind, and it is a rhythm, not
a setting: open a scene a notch cooler, tighten as pressure rises, and let the chapter's sharpest
beat be its closest prose. Thought reaches the page **inside the narration**, in the character's own
idiom and unmarked (free indirect), so it needs no "she thought" and no filter between the reader
and the thing perceived. Everyone else in the scene thinks through what they do first.

## Examples

### Distance moving through one beat

> Tovi came down the ladder to Deck Four with the clamp kit on her shoulder and found the main
> already frosting. Two fitters stood well back from it.
>
> Hiss, high and thin. Wrong gauge on the clamp, she could see that from here. Fine. Borrow the
> other one off Ossie's belt and pray he'd actually brought it.

The first paragraph places us: who, where, what is wrong. The second has no seam between prose and
Tovi's mind, because the pressure has arrived. The prose did not announce the change; the
sentences did it, by getting shorter and taking on her words ("Fine", "pray he'd actually").

### Filtered and direct

**Filtered: the reader watches Tovi perceive:**

> She saw that the deck alarm light was already amber, and she felt a cold certainty that Kell had
> known about the leak for days. She realised he had wanted her to find it.

**Direct: the reader perceives:**

> The deck alarm light was already amber. Kell had known about this leak for days. Of course he
> had. He'd wanted her to be the one to find it.

*Saw*, *felt*, *realised* each put a narrator between the reader and the thing. Keep one only when
the act of perceiving *is* the event: *She watched him sign it and said nothing.*

### Direct thought, rare and at a decision

> The clerk turned the form round and tapped the empty line marked Guardian.
> 'Don't let him fill it in.'
> "I'll bring it back tomorrow," Tovi said.

One marked thought, at the moment of choice, with no "she thought" (the marks already say whose it
is). One to three a chapter. Past that, marked thought stops being emphatic and becomes captions.

### Other people's minds, through their first move

> Three of them came into the shift office together. Kell looked at the clock. Ossie looked at
> Tovi. The clerk looked at the door they'd come in by, and kept looking at it.

There is one viewpoint and three minds, and nobody's head was entered.

## When to break it

A cool, outside distance held for a whole scene is a choice when the character is numb: after a
blow lands, pulling back *is* the feeling. Make the choice, then come back in.
