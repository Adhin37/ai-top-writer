# Switching viewpoint

Only the characters `novel.md` lists under `pov.pov_characters` may hold the viewpoint, and they
switch only where `pov.switch_granularity` allows: at a chapter break, or at a scene break, once a
chapter at most. Never inside a scene: two heads in one scene is head-hopping. Each viewpoint
narrates in its own idiom, drawing its comparisons from its own life. The reader should know whose
head this is from the first sentence after the break, without being told a name.

## Examples

**A switch that has to announce itself:**

> * * *
>
> Meanwhile, in the supervisor's office, Kell was thinking about the leak.

**A switch the prose makes on its own:**

> * * *
>
> Six shifts of logs, and the girl had found the only one he'd signed. Kell folded the rag in
> thirds, then in thirds again, and put it in the drawer with the others.

The rag and the counting are his; the reader knows whose head this is before the name. Where
Tovi's chapters reach for pipes and gauges, his reach for paper.

**Head-hopping: two minds in one scene:**

> Tovi was sure he was lying. Kell, for his part, wondered how much she had seen.

Keep one head, and let the other mind show in what it does first: *Kell looked at the drawer
before he looked at her.*

**The antagonist's viewpoint is spent, not given.** A chapter in the opponent's head trades menace
for understanding. Spend it at the moment the reader should stop being afraid of them and start
being afraid for someone else.

## When to break it

A mind that is not human (a construct, an animal, a god) may narrate if `novel.md` lists it, and
then its blind spots are the prose's: a mind with no concept of lying reports what was said and
done, and the reader sees the lie it cannot.
