# Openings and endings

A serial is read a chapter at a time, days apart, by someone following six other stories, so every
chapter re-earns its reader twice. The **opening** starts in motion and carries any recap in one
clause of a sentence doing other work. The **ending** stops on the last strong beat, not the
wind-down after it, and leaves an unresolved pressure; the beat sheet's `hook` names its kind.
A hook is not a cliffhanger, and it is not the same shape every week.

## Examples

### Openings

**A start that throat-clears:**

> The morning shift bell rang at six, as it always did. Tovi woke in the family bunk, thinking
> about the day before, when Supervisor Kell had struck her grade for the leak on Deck Four.

**In motion, with the recap folded into it:**

> The recruitment office was a door on Deck Seven with no sign, and the queue outside it was
> everybody Kell had ever struck off.

The second carries the same facts (her grade is gone, she has to do something about it) inside a
sentence that has already moved on. Other openings that work: a line of dialogue already in
conflict, a wrong detail stated flatly, or the aftermath first and the cause filled in during the
scene.

### Endings: stop at the beat

**Three paragraphs past the stopping point:**

> She signed the slip. The recruiter took it and filed it with the others.
>
> On the way back up she thought about her brother, and about what she would tell him, and about
> how strange it was that one signature could change so much. The ladders seemed longer than
> usual. She was tired.

**Stopped at the beat:**

> She signed the slip. The recruiter took it, read her name, and looked up.
> "Brand," he said. "You'd be the second one."

The second leaves a concrete question (second what? who was the first?) and nothing after it.

### Kinds of ending

The beat sheet's `hook` line says which kind this chapter's is. A **reveal** (new information
reframes what came before), an **arrival**, a **decision** whose consequence the reader wants, a
**question** (a fact that does not fit), a **threat** (a clock starts), a **reversal**, a
**cliff** (physical peril, rare), or **quiet** (a line that lands, with no question at all, after a
heavy chapter).

Make the ending concrete. "Something was wrong" is not a hook; "His father's name was the first line
in the ledger" is. And deliver what it implied next chapter: a hook that promises a fight and opens
on a conversation works once.

### The same shape, week after week

> The door stayed shut.
> The slip stayed in her boot.
> Nobody came.

A short, withheld line of narration with nobody speaking is a good last line once. Three chapters
running, the reader feels the sameness before they can name it. End some chapters on a line of
speech, some on an action, some mid-scene.

## When to break it

A chapter that closes an arc, or follows a death, can end on the wind-down: the reader needs the
quiet as much as the next question. That is the `quiet` ending, chosen, about once an arc.
