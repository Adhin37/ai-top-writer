# The world on the page

The bible says what is true; the page decides what reaches the reader. A world is cheapest and most
vivid as **consequence** (a rule bites someone), **friction** (someone works around it) or
**assumed reference** (someone mentions it as obvious), and most expensive as description. Even good
description fails when the place only washes over the character. Ask what their hands or feet dealt
with: picked up, pushed past, paid for, climbed.

## Examples

### Felt and handled

**Felt: vivid, and nobody does anything with it:**

> The pump hall was hot and close, and the air tasted of oil and old metal. The great pistons rose
> and fell in the dim amber light, and their slow rhythm settled over Tovi like a held breath.

**Handled: the same hall, in somebody's way:**

> Tovi went along the pump hall sideways, shoulder to the rail, because the pistons on Deck Nine
> came down hard enough to take a careless elbow off. She had to shout her name to the duty clerk
> twice over the noise, and he wrote it wrong anyway.

The second has fewer sensory words and more world: the danger, the noise, the clerk, a place built
for machines and not for people.

### Four ways one fact reaches the page

The fact: on the Stack, your air is metered and billed every shift.

```
description   On the Stack, every resident's air was metered and billed by shift.
consequence   Her meter ticked over at the shift bell, and for the first time it was billing her.
friction      She held her breath down the long ladder to Deck Five. It was stupid, and she did it anyway.
reference     "Breathe on your own credit," Kell said, which on the Stack was how you told someone to leave.
```

Reach for description only when the other three cannot carry it, and then once, plainly.

### Perception is character

Nobody perceives a room. A particular person notices what their life makes them notice:

> In the canteen Ossie saw the food. Tovi saw that the vent over the third table was taped shut,
> and that nobody sat under it.

And nobody notices what is normal to them. A native of the Stack does not register the hiss of the
vents; a newcomer cannot stop hearing it.

### Depth by reserve

A world feels large in proportion to what it declines to explain. Once a chapter or so, let someone
refer to a custom, debt or event as obvious and never gloss it:

> "Not after the Ninth-Deck business," the clerk said, and that settled it.

If the reader needs it later, it will come back as a consequence.

## When to break it

An establishing passage is a choice when a place is about to matter and the reader has never been
there: the first time the deep crews go down, say. Make it short, make it somebody's first look,
and let it end on something they have to do.
