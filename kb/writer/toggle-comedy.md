# Comic beats

Humour comes from **characters**, not from the narrator: who is funny, in what register, and above
all what they joke to avoid. Who may be funny at all is in `bible/cast/_voices.md` (the `wit`
axis); never hand a joke to someone whose row says none. A funny beat works by contrast, before
the pressure or inside it as someone's way of coping. The test: after it, does the reader still
believe the protagonist could lose?

## Examples

**The narrator winking at the reader:**

> The meeting was, as meetings on the Stack tended to be, a masterpiece of saying nothing at great
> length.

**A character coping, and raising the tension:**

> "If I'm shipped down-well," Tovi said, "I want it noted I was right about the clamp."
> "Noted," said the clerk, and wrote it down. That was worse.

The joke is hers and it is about the thing she is afraid of. The clerk taking it literally turns
it, so the scene got more dangerous, not less.

**Specificity is most of comedy.** The wrong detail, delivered flat:

> The deep-crew recruitment poster had been updated. The number of crews returned had been
> painted over, neatly, in the same green as the wall.

**Undersell.** Nobody laughs to cue the reader, and the funny word comes last in the sentence.

## When to break it

Right after a death or a real loss, no jokes from the narrator and none from anyone who did not
know the dead. The one who did may make the worst joke of the book, and it should land like a
blow.
