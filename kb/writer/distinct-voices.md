# Distinct voices

The cast sounds alike when one mind wears several names, and it happens when the speakers are
chosen, not when the lines are written. Each speaker's row in `bible/cast/_voices.md` says how
quickly they think, how well they say it, whether they joke, how hot they run, how long a turn
usually is, what their hands do and how a turn is built. Hold those numbers. Intelligence and
articulacy are independent: someone right and unable to prove it, and someone wrong and convincing,
are both people.

## Examples

### One fact, three voices

The fact: the pump on Deck Nine is failing.

> **Kell** (sharp, articulate, flat; long, even turns): "The Deck Nine pump has run outside
> tolerance for six shifts. I've logged it. Engineering will schedule it when they schedule it, and
> until then we don't speak about it on open channels."
>
> **The old fitter** (sharp, inarticulate, quick; four words at a time): "Nine's going. Hear it?
> Listen. There."
>
> **Ossie** (ordinary, eager, warm; talks past his point): "Is that the one that does the thing?
> Because my bunkmate said there's a smell on Nine, like a burnt smell, but he says that about the
> canteen too, so."

Cover the names and you still know who spoke: by the length of the turn, whether a reason is
given, and what each one does when they reach the edge of what they know.

### Two voices that collapsed into one

> "We should check the log before we accuse anyone," Tovi said.
> "We should check the log before we act," Ossie agreed. "Anything else would be careless."

Ossie has borrowed Tovi's sentence shape and her caution. Give him his own:

> "We should check the log before we accuse anyone," Tovi said.
> "Or we could just ask him. He's right there."

### A child is a different architecture

> **Flat:** "I don't understand why you have to go down-well. It seems unfair to me."
>
> **Nine years old:** "You said thirty days. It's been six. You said thirty."

A child's sentences have one clause, live in the present, and argue by repetition and by
fairness. The second line is not simpler words; it is a different way of building a turn.

### Turn length is the cheapest separator

If one person always answers in four words and another in forty, a reader knows them by the shape
of the paragraph before reading it. Keep each speaker's usual length from their row, and let them
break it only when something breaks them.

## When to break it

A copy of a person (a clone, a double, someone wearing another's face) may share the original's
voice; that is the premise. Let the copy drift as it lives a different life, and let someone in the
scene notice the first word the original would never have used.
