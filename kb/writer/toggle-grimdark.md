# Consequences that stay

The promise to the reader is that what happens stays happened. Named people die, sometimes at
inconvenient moments and to no purpose; injuries take chapters and some do not heal; victories are
partial; and nobody turns out to have been secretly alive. It stays readable only if something the
reader wants protected is still standing, and competence still improves the odds.

## Examples

**Grief told at length:**

> Tovi mourned Ossie for days. The loss weighed on her constantly, a grey heaviness that coloured
> everything, and she wondered if she would ever feel whole again.

**Grief as the wrong practical detail:**

> Ossie's kit was still on the hook by the door, with the clamp in it he'd borrowed and never given
> back. She used the clamp for a month before she noticed she'd stopped thinking of it as his.

**An injury that stays:**

> She could close the left hand now, mostly. She learned to carry the kit on the right shoulder and
> to let Doran take the ladders first, and she hated both.

**A moral cost the story does not forgive:**

> Doran never said anything about the chit she'd signed. He just stopped asking her to take the
> early shift with him, and she never asked why.

## When to break it

A small mercy that costs the giver something and holds is load-bearing here, not a softening:
it is what makes the next loss land. Keep at least one in every few chapters.
