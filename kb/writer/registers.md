# Registers

A register is how the narration sounds: sentence length, how close it sits to the protagonist,
how much attitude it has, how much it lets the reader see. Three samples of one beat let a user
choose a voice by reading it. Asked for three, a model writes one register three times, with
different adjectives. Make them differ in **kind**: change the distance, the sentence shape and the
narrator's attitude, keep the events and the rule landing the same. One of the three is plain
enough for a reader who has never seen this world.

## Example

The world is invented: a sealed habitat where every breath is billed by job grade. The beat: at
shift change, Tovi's meter turns red, the day her grade was struck.

**A — plain and quick**

> The meter on Tovi's wrist clicked at shift change, the way it did for everyone. This time the
> number went red. Ungraded meant the air was hers to pay for now: every breath, billed by the
> shift. She had thirty days of credit. After that, down-well. She did the sum twice. It came out
> the same.

**B — close and textured**

> Shift bell. The meter gave its small dry tick, and the light under the glass went from green to
> the colour of a burn. Tovi felt it before she read it, a tightness, as if the pipe in the wall
> had heard. Thirty days. She breathed in carefully, the way the old fitters did on the ladders,
> and caught herself doing it.

**C — wry, with a narrator who has opinions**

> On the Stack they don't take your air away. That would be rude. They start sending you the bill.
> Tovi's came at shift change, a polite red glow on her wrist, and with it thirty days of credit,
> which management called generous and the fitters called the long walk. She was already counting
> breaths. Old habit. Bad sign.

All three land the same rule: air is billed, the grade decided who paid, thirty days, then
down-well. A is the easiest to follow cold; B costs speed and buys feeling; C buys a voice and
costs the reader's trust when things turn grim.

## Check

Read the three first sentences in a row. If they could be one writer on three different days,
they are one register: change the distance or the attitude, not the words.

## When to break it

If `novel.md` already fixes the narration (first person, present tense), keep it in all three and
vary the rest.
