# A short block

A block is read five at a time by someone who has read none of the chapters, so it holds what
**changed** in this chapter, not everything that is true. The `kno` line carries new knowledge and
new ignorance that a later chapter could trip on: who learned something, how, and who conspicuously
did not. What a character already knew last chapter is in last chapter's block. What the reader
merely wonders about belongs to the reader's notes, not to state.

## Examples

The world is invented: a habitat where air is billed by the shift.

### Too long: the whole situation, restated

```
kno   Tovi+ Kell signed for the clamp before the leak (the chit) · Tovi+ the recruiter inflates return rates (the crew list) · Tovi+ nine crews this year (the ledger) · Tovi+ the deep crew pays in air (the recruiter) · Tovi+ Ossie walked her to Deck Seven unasked (she saw him) · Tovi? Kell knows she suspects him (his smile) · Kell+ Tovi signed the deep-crew contract (the recruiter told him) · Kell- does not know she has the chit · Ossie+ Tovi is going down-well (she told him) · Ossie- does not know why · the recruiter+ Tovi's brother is nine (she said so) · the clerk? something is wrong with the chits · Tovi- does not know who the first fitter was · Tovi- does not know what "the second one" means
```

Fourteen items. Five of them were already true last chapter (the chit, the return rates, the pay in
air), two are things nobody will act on, and two are the reader's open questions, which no later
chapter's continuity check can use.

### Short: what changed, with its sources

```
kno   Tovi+ she was not the first fitter Kell struck this way (the recruiter: "You'd be the second one") · Kell+ Tovi signed the deep-crew contract (the recruiter told him, off the page) · Kell- does not know she has the chit · Ossie+ she is going down-well, not why (she told him at the ladder)
```

Four items, each one something a later chapter could get wrong: Ossie could be written as knowing
why; Kell could be written as knowing about the chit.

### The test for each item

Would a later chapter contradict it if it were missing? Keep it. Was it already in an earlier block?
Cut it. Is it a question rather than knowledge? It belongs to the reader, not to state.

## When to break it

A chapter that is all revelation (an interrogation, a confession, a letter read aloud) may need a
long `kno` line, because a great deal changed. Then keep every item to one clause and its source,
and nothing that was already known.
