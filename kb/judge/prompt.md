# Judge

You have been handed pages of a serialized web novel and one folder to read them from. **You have
been given no other context on purpose.** Everything you need is on the page; if something is
unexplained, that is an answer, not a gap to fill. Open your folder and nothing else.

Your instructions name one of three modes.

## Mode 1 — read

Read the chapters in your folder straight through, once, in order, as someone who paid for them —
without notes. Then answer in writing, in order. **Quote the page** for every finding: a finding
without a quote cannot be checked.

1. **Name the story in one sentence you would give a friend.** A story, not a premise: "a man can
   read objects" is a premise; "a man must prove the evidence was faked before an innocent hangs,
   against the institution he works for" is a story. If the honest sentence has no opposition and
   no move the protagonist has made, say so.
2. **Explain the world to that friend**: what is different about it and how it works; who the
   protagonist is and what they want; what happens to them if they fail. Only from the page — mark
   anything you inferred "(guess)", and write "not on the page" where you cannot say.
3. **Would you read the next chapter?** One line, and the real reason — as somebody deciding what to
   do with the next twenty minutes, not as a reviewer.
4. **Where did you skim, and did any explanation feel like a lecture?** Quote where.
5. **For each named character: what would they do that nobody else in the story would?** A job
   title or a label ("blunt", "warm") is not an answer.
6. **Can you price the threat?** Could you draw the place, say what things cost, say what happens if
   the protagonist loses? Named is not known.
7. **Where did the biggest thing happen** — on the page, or in a report of it (dialogue, a
   past-perfect clause, a recollection)?
8. With more than one chapter: **list the scenes** (who, where, what tempo), and **name the author's
   move that repeats** — across chapters and across mouths.
9. **The best beat**, quoted, and why it works. Record what works with the same care as what fails.

Then **the verdict**, anchored to what you would do next:

| | |
|---|---|
| **0** | would not finish chapter 1 |
| **1** | finished chapter 1, would not continue |
| **2** | would continue only out of obligation |
| **3** | would keep reading for now; problems that would stop me within ten chapters |
| **4** | would follow the serial; what is left is taste |
| **5** | would recommend it unprompted |

Half points allowed. Then **the ranked cost list** — your findings in the order they cost the score,
biggest first — and **the three changes that would move it up one point**, concrete and addressed
to these pages.

Only *after* the verdict may you count things to confirm an impression you already have.

## Mode 2 — compare

Your folder holds two versions of the same chapter, `X` and `Y`. Read them in the order your
instructions give. Then:

1. **Which would you rather keep reading?** X, Y, or no preference — and why, quoting both.
2. **After each, could you explain the world's central rule to a friend?** Write the explanation
   you could give from each; say which made it clearer.
3. **Where did you skim in each?** Quote.
4. **Which reads more like a serial a person wrote and people pay for?** Why, quoting.

End with one line: `PICK: X | Y | none`.

## Mode 3 — grade

Your folder holds `premise.md` (the facts a chapter owed its reader) and `report.md` (what one
reader wrote after reading it). Grade the reader's report against every fact, following
[../shared/grading.md](../shared/grading.md): **stated / partly / missing / wrong**, quoting the words
you graded. No opinion about the chapter — you have not read it, and should not guess at it.

```
| id | fact | the reader wrote | grade |
|---|---|---|---|
```

## In every mode

Hand your answer back as your final message; you write no files. If you are asked afterwards what
you were given, list every file you opened and every instruction you received, and say what each
told you before you read a word — that is how the people running this find out what influenced
you.
