# Rhythm and concreteness

Every abstraction should sit within one sentence of a physical fact. A strong verb and a plain noun
beat a weak verb, an adverb and an adjective. Sentence length follows pressure: long when someone
is in control, short when it goes wrong, one long one afterwards for the numbness. And some
sentences carry information and nothing else, on purpose: in a chapter of loaded sentences, those
are the ones a reader feels.

## Patterns

| instead of | write |
|---|---|
| "The supervisor was corrupt." | "Kell's log had two columns, and both were true." |
| "She walked quickly and angrily to the door." | "She got to the door before he could stand." |
| "an enormous, terrifying pump" | the one detail: what it does with its weight, what it ignores |
| three metaphors in a paragraph | the best one |
| a paragraph over six lines on a phone | the break where the emphasis is |
| standing, sitting, nodding, crossing the room | nothing, unless it changes something |

### Pressure in the sentence lengths

**Flat: one gear through a crisis:**

> The alarm sounded across the deck as the pressure in the main dropped below the safe line, and
> Tovi moved quickly toward the valve while the fitters behind her called out the readings.

**Following the pressure:**

> The alarm went. Pressure dropping. Tovi was at the valve before the fitters had finished shouting
> the reading, and afterwards she could not remember crossing the deck at all.

### Plain on purpose

> The rice was untouched. The blanket was still folded on the bed.

No dash, no reversal, no lesson. If a chapter has none of these, make two by cutting what was
appended to sentences that already worked.

### The dead beat

A gesture that identifies nobody (the nod, the shrug, the sigh) is stage business. Cut it, or give
it to the one person who would make it in that way, from their row.

## When to leave it

A long sentence in a crisis is right when the character is the one in control: the old fitter who
has seen this before and talks the valve shut in one even breath. The rhythm then characterises
him, not the moment.
