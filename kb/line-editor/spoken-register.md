# Spoken register

Dialogue can be the right length for the chapter and still be nobody talking: every line a finished
sentence, nobody cut off, three paragraphs of narration between one line and the next. Lint's
`stats` line shows the speech share; the rest you hear. You change how a line is said, never what it
says, and each speaker stays inside their row in `bible/cast/_voices.md`.

## Patterns

| instead of | write |
|---|---|
| one turn carrying the whole reasoning | a short statement, a beat of business, then the honest part |
| every line reaching a full stop | an answer of two words, and one that stops early |
| a line, three paragraphs of thought, a line | the lines side by side, so two people actually talk |
| everyone allowed to finish | someone cutting in, someone answering the question they wish they'd been asked |
| no contractions in an ordinary mouth | contractions, unless the row says formal |

### A speech, made spoken

**Before:**

> "I understand that you are upset about the grade, and I want you to know that I did not make the
> decision lightly, because the regulations require me to act when a fault is caused by negligence."

**After, with the same content, said by the same person:**

> "You're upset. I understand." Kell folded the rag. "It wasn't light. Negligence is negligence."

Nothing he says has changed. Only the shape has: three turns where there was one, and a pause
filled with his hands.

### Narration between the lines

> "Fine," she said.
> She thought about the clamp, and about the hour on the chit, and about what it would mean if
> Kell had known all along, and about her brother, who would ask tonight.
> "Then I'll take the deep crew."

If the thinking is the scene, keep it and cut it to the one thought that turns her. If it is the
narrator explaining the next line, cut it: the second line says it.

## When to leave it

A character whose row makes them formal, long-winded or rehearsed keeps it: the official reading a
judgement, the man who prepared his speech. Leave it in their mouth, and make sure someone else in
the scene sounds different.
