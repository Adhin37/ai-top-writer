# Machine-default habits

None of these is wrong once. Each becomes a fingerprint when it is the chapter's default move, and
together they make a narrator with one attitude: every sentence loaded, every scene closed on a
small ironic withholding, nothing ever allowed to just say a thing. The fix is not deletion — a
chapter with every habit removed and nothing added is emptier, not better. **Make some sentences
plain on purpose**: something described because it is there, a line said and meant.

## The habits

| habit | looks like | thin it to |
|---|---|---|
| **The antithesis** | "It was weather, not prophecy." · "Not relief. Something smaller." | the one or two that carry real contrast |
| **The dash appositive** | a clause, a dash, then a phrase re-explaining it: "she paid the toll — the small, automatic courtesy of someone who no longer argued" | say it once; keep dashes for interruption |
| **The aphoristic close** | a scene ends on a portable lesson: "A promise kept was one data point. It was not yet a pattern." | end most scenes on an action or a line of speech |
| **The retrospective tag** | "…, which was its own kind of answer." · "…, which was exactly the point." | cut the tag; the sentence before it already said it |
| **The abstract as object** | "turning three years of careful arithmetic over in her hands" | a concrete thing in the hands, or nothing |
| **The stage direction** | "A beat." · "A pause." written into the prose | an action, or nothing |
| **The triple hedge** | "she did not hear — could not have heard, did not need to —" | commit to one |
| **The rule of three** | "It was cold, it was quiet, and it was wrong." | two, or four; three is the default cadence |
| **The default gestures** | nodding, shrugging, sighing, raised eyebrows, released breaths | a gesture this character would make, or none |
| **The echoed phrase** | the same simile or image in several scenes ("soft as cloth" three times) | keep the best one |
| **The explained subtext** | a marked thought restating what the scene just showed | cut the thought; trust the scene |

## Stock phrases — cut on sight

*a testament to* · *something shifted* · *the weight of X settled over* · *a moment that stretched*
· *she couldn't shake the feeling* · *little did she know* · *for the first time in a long time* ·
*a silence stretched between them* · *the air was thick with tension* · *a chill ran down his
spine* · *time seemed to slow* · *a breath she didn't know she was holding* · *this changes
everything* · *his expression changed drastically* · *in the next instant*

## Plain on purpose — what the fix looks like

> The rice was untouched. The blanket was still folded on the bed.

Two sentences that carry information and nothing else — no dash, no reversal, no lesson. In a
chapter of loaded sentences, these are the ones a reader feels. Roughly a third of a chapter can
work like this.

## When to leave it

A habit that belongs to one character's voice — the lecturer's triads, the pedant's dashes — is
characterisation. Leave it in their mouth and thin it everywhere else.
