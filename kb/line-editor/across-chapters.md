# Habits across chapters

Read the two chapters before this one beside it. A move used once in each chapter is invisible to
any check of one chapter, and a reader still feels it by the third: the same gesture, the same image,
the same piece of wisdom in a new mouth, the same shape of last line. Thin it in this chapter.
Keep it only if it is a deliberate callback, one whose second use means something the first did
not. Report each as an `across` line.

## Patterns

### The signature gesture

> Ch 1: "Kell folded the rag in thirds."
> Ch 2: "He folded the rag in thirds and set it down."
> Ch 3: "Kell folded his rag, thirds, and looked at her."

A character tic is a fingerprint; three verbatim uses is the drafter's habit. Keep the tic, and
vary how it lands or cut one.

```
across  "folded the rag in thirds" ch 1, ch 2 — thinned here: "put the rag away without looking at it"
```

### The aphorism in every mouth

> Ch 1, Kell: "A log is only as good as the hand that signs it."
> Ch 2, the clerk: "A chit is only as good as the hand that signs it."
> Ch 3, Tovi: "A grade's only as good as the hand that strikes it."

One line of wisdom has become the book's catchphrase, and three different people say it. Keep it
in the mouth it belongs to, and cut it from the rest.

### The same last line

Three chapters ending on a short withheld sentence of narration with nobody speaking. Check this
chapter's last line against the last two, and against the *Ending* of the beat sheet
(`work/chNNNN/beats.md`). If the beat sheet names that last beat, it is the story's: keep what it
says and change its shape, by putting it in a mouth or in an action, or by moving it a line
earlier. If the beat sheet does not name it, end on the line of speech or the action just before
it.

### The callback that earns it

> Ch 1: Kell gives her the chit and does not let go of it.
> Ch 3: She gives it back to him, and he cannot let go of it.

The second use reverses the first. Keep it, and say so:

```
across  "did not let go of it" ch 1, ch 3 — kept: the reversal is the point
```

## When to leave it

A refrain the story makes on purpose (a prayer, an oath, a line from a song) recurs by design, and
so does the last beat the beat sheet names. If the beat sheet or the bible names it, you may
reshape it but not remove it.
