# Cost, and what is worth the price

Nothing is free: every chapter takes something, from an hour or a lie up to a person or a place.
And something has to be worth the price, or the reader is paying instalments on a thing they were
never shown. Both pass the same test: a reader can point at them in one concrete sentence. A cost
paid in an earlier chapter is still being paid.

## Patterns

### The cost is a mood

> Cost: "Tovi suffers."

Fails. "Tovi can't go back to the fitters' bunk, and her brother is still on its roster" passes.
Ask what she no longer has, or can no longer do.

### The cost is paid by somebody offstage

The chapter's loss lands on a character the reader has never met, reported in a line. Nothing was
risked by anyone the reader is following, so nothing was paid.

### Nothing to keep

A chapter that only takes reads as relentless, not tense. The thing worth the price is small, real
and usually unearned:

> Ossie had kept her seat in the canteen, which he'd been doing for nine days and neither of them
> had mentioned.

The strongest version arrives while the chapter is about something else. It is not a reward for
winning; a chapter that pays out only on success teaches the reader that warmth is a scoreboard.
If the beat sheet names a `keeps` and the draft lost it, that is a note.

### The healed wound

> Chapter 3: she cannot close her left hand. Chapter 4: she climbs the ladder two rungs at a time.

An injury, exhaustion, a broken trust or a debt from an earlier chapter is gone without a scene that
spent it. Check the last block's `cost` line in `state/continuity.md` against the draft.

```
N3 the hand healed between chapters
where "took the ladder two rungs at a time"
ev    state C0003 cost: "no grip in her left hand for days" · own reading: one day later
eff   a reader who remembers the frostbite stops trusting that anything here costs
```

### The free win

> The chief's son sneered at her grade. She named the fault in his pump before he finished, the
> hall went quiet, and he apologised.

Nothing was paid and no enemy was made, so the next chapter needs another insult and another win:
a loop that stops the story. A note asks for one of these: the win costs her something; the insult
was partly true; winning makes a real enemy; she refuses the fight and the reader sees what that
cost; nobody in the hall cares.

### The violence that costs nobody

Someone hurt a person and the chapter moved on. Whoever did it carries it: in their sleep, their
hands, what they will not do next time.

## When it does not apply

`novel.md` → `tone.warmth: cold` stands the "worth the price" half down: a story whose subject is
that nobody is safe may have nothing to keep. The cost half never stands down.
